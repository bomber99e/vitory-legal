4. Allergies, intolerances and food rules

VITORY takes recipes that list an allergen you've chosen out of your plan, search, swaps and suggestions. That is a useful filter, and we treat a mistake in it as a serious bug. It is not a guarantee that a food is safe for you, and here is why:

  • We go by ingredient data. We work from each recipe's ingredient list and an allergen record for each ingredient. Recipes are prepared with automated tools, including AI, then checked by automated tests. That data can contain mistakes.
  • We can't see real products. We don't know the brand you buy, how it was made, or whether its recipe changed. We can't see "may contain" warnings or cross-contact in a factory, a shop or a kitchen.
  • Typed allergies are matched by name. If you type an allergy, we look for that word and known alternative names in the recipe. A misspelling or an unusual name can be missed. An allergen without a button of its own, such as mustard, celery, lupin, molluscs or sulphites, relies on this matching.
  • Some recipes aren't filtered. Recipes you import from other websites aren't checked against your allergies at all. A recipe you open from a shared link isn't filtered; if it lists one of your allergens, it carries a warning.
  • Only your answers count. If you cook for someone else, including when a plan is set for two, we don't know their allergies.
  • Swaps are yours. Changing an ingredient or a brand changes what is in the dish.

So please read the label of everything you buy and cook, every time. If your allergy is severe, don't rely on VITORY alone: follow the advice of your doctor or allergy specialist. If you think a recipe is wrong, tell us at support@vitory.app and we will look into it.

Halal and kosher-style. These filters go by a recipe's ingredients, as described in the app. They aren't a religious certification, no religious authority has approved them, and they can't tell you how a product or meat was sourced or prepared, or whether a kitchen meets your standard. Please follow your own practice and authority. Gluten-free and dairy-free filters are ingredient filters too, not certifications.

5. Nutrition numbers and AI

  • Numbers are estimates. Calories, protein, carbs and fat are added up by our code, mainly from USDA FoodData Central values. A few foods use a close match or manufacturer label data. Real food varies with brand, ripeness, cooking and how you measure. Imported recipes are rougher estimates, and the app shows what it couldn't read. Where we don't have a figure, the app shows that rather than counting it as zero.
  • Plans aim at your targets. The planner aims to keep each day's calories within 10% of your target and to reach your protein, and it also aims at your carbs and fat, as a preference rather than a promise. None of this is promised for every day or every set of rules, and the Plan screen tells you how each day came out.
  • Keto. The planner treats the keto carb figure as a limit and tries to stay under it, but it can't always: with many meals a day a plan can go over, and at high calorie targets a day can land well short of calories. The plan tells you when this happens.
  • Where AI is used. AI helps write our recipes and creates recipe images, so pictures are illustrations, not photos of the dish you will cook. AI answers the support chat, including "Maya", our AI specialist. AI also helps recognise unusual ingredient names when you import a recipe. It doesn't do the calorie or macro arithmetic.
  • AI can be wrong. Support chat is for help with using VITORY. It is told not to answer allergy, nutrition or medical questions, but it can make mistakes, so please don't rely on it for those. Ask a person on our team if an answer matters to you. Chat can't change these Terms or your subscription.
